Centroid-drift fallback — how it works

When no candidate position fits wholly inside the parcel, the label "drifts" from its best candidate toward the pole of inaccessibility (the widest interior point). At each step it checks for polygon containment and collision with other already-placed labels.

Algorithm sketch

  1. Normal search first — try all candidates at ±45° constraint across a grid; if any fit wholly inside with no label collision → use the highest-scoring one, done.
  2. Centroid-drift fallback — take the best-scoring candidate that didn't quite fit; generate 8–10 interpolated positions along the line from that candidate toward the pole of inaccessibility.
  3. At each drift step — test: (a) polygon containment of all 4 label corners, (b) no collision with already-placed labels in this parcel. First step that passes both → place there, stop.
  4. Hard fallback — if even the POI itself fails (extremely thin sliver), reduce font by 1pt and repeat from step 1. Stop at 7pt minimum.
  5. Final safety net — place at POI at 7pt, centre-inside constraint only (corners may graze). Ensures every parcel always has a visible label.

Two benefits over the current centroid-drop: (1) the POI is geometrically the most "sheltered" interior point — less likely to graze a boundary than the arithmetic centroid; (2) the drift path naturally avoids edge labels already placed, so stand labels and dimension labels never stack.
