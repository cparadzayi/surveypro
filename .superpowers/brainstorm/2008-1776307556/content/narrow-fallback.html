Very narrow parcels — what happens when nothing fits?

When a parcel is so thin that even a ±45° rotated label at minimum font can't fit wholly inside, we need a fallback strategy. Three options below — each shown on the same extremely narrow parcel.

A

Shrink font to hard minimum (7pt), always contained

Keep reducing font size until the label fits wholly inside at the best ±45° angle. 7pt is the floor — below that it's unreadable in the field anyway. Very thin slivers may show tiny text but it will always be inside the parcel.

B

Centroid fallback — place at centre, accept minor clip (current)

If nothing fits, render at centroid anyway. The label may slightly cross a boundary. Current behaviour — familiar but violates the "wholly contained" requirement for extreme cases.

C

Leader line — dot inside parcel, label text outside

A small dot marks the parcel centre; a thin line leads to the label placed clear of all boundaries. Common in dense cadastral plans overseas. More complex to implement but very clean on-plan.

D

Best-effort: label centre inside parcel, corners may graze boundary

Place the label so its centre point is inside the parcel at the best ±45° angle. If the label corners touch or just cross the boundary, that's acceptable — the text reads clearly and is visually associated with the correct parcel. Practical compromise for slivers.

This only affects extreme slivers — the majority of parcels will find a clean fit under the ±45° rule with the improved placement search.
