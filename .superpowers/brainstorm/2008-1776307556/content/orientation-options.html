Beacon label orientation — how strict should the east-west constraint be?

The current algorithm uses two candidate angles: 0° (horizontal) and the parcel's longest-edge angle. For narrow parcels oriented north–south, this produces near-vertical labels. We need to choose how tightly to constrain the allowed angle range.

A

Strict horizontal only (0°)

Labels are always east-west, no exceptions. Narrow parcels get progressively smaller font until the label fits horizontally, or fall back to a minimum readable size (e.g. 7pt). Maximally consistent; some very narrow parcels may show small text.

B

Capped tilt — max ±30° from horizontal

Allows a gentle lean to follow the parcel shape, but never goes past 30° (so labels never read "uphill" or "downhill"). Wider parcels get 0°; narrow diagonal parcels get up to 30°. Best balance between readability and parcel-following.

C

Capped tilt — max ±45° from horizontal

More permissive. Labels can tilt up to 45° to follow diagonal parcels while still staying in the "readable" half of the compass. Same as Option B but with a wider band — gives more candidates for very narrow diagonal parcels.

Note: the polygon-containment and no-overlap-with-boundary constraints apply equally under all three options — the only difference is the allowed angle range for the stand number label.
