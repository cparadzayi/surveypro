Placing suffix labels in wider whitespace — maintaining corner association

For narrow parcels like 2031–2034, the corner ends are the tightest spots. The label should travel toward the widest interior zone. When it moves far from its beacon circle, we need a way to keep the association clear.

A

Always use a leader line when label drifts from corner

Any time the label moves more than a threshold distance from its beacon circle, a thin dashed line connects them. Clean and unambiguous — the surveyor always knows which corner the label belongs to. Common in dense urban cadastral plans.

B

No leader lines — place label in widest zone, rely on proximity

Label always lands in the widest available whitespace. No extra lines drawn. Simpler on the plan but can become ambiguous when multiple beacons are close together or when the label drifts far from its corner.

C

Adaptive — short push stays at corner (no leader), long drift gets a leader line

If the label fits close to its corner (within ~1.5× the beacon spacing), place it there with no leader. If it has to travel far into the interior to find whitespace, add a thin dashed leader back to the beacon circle. Best of both: clean plans where possible, unambiguous where needed.
